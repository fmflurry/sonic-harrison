Pixel art, the spin ball, Nox, and two-minute levels

Piko Turbo now looks like one 16-bit game. Every sprite and tile is hand-authored pixel data in the code. Piko curls into a ball, Nox joins as a second hero with a dash, and the five levels each last about two minutes. Phaser and its CDN script are gone, so the game makes no network call at all.

How a frame is drawn

1. Pixel datajs/sprites/*.js: palettes and rows of characters, one per pixel
▶
2. Pre-render oncejs/art.js: sprite canvases, sky, parallax, loops, terrain chunks
▶
3. Art bufferjs/render.js: 480×270, positions rounded to whole art pixels
▶
4. Screenjs/play.js: one upscale at device pixels, imageSmoothingEnabled = false

Rules that changed

Ball, like the 16-bit gamesEvery jump curls into a ball (hitbox 34 instead of 48). Above 400 px/s on the ground (boost pad, momentum down a slope, out of a loop) he curls up by himself, rolls with low friction and speeds up downhill, then stands up once back near running speed. Running downhill now builds momentum too. No Boule button; the keyboard down arrow still curls on purpose.
Blob popA ball or a dash pops a blob for 4 s; it comes back only when the player is 240 px away. Touching one standing still returns to the flag, rings kept.
NoxTop speed 290 instead of 260. Éclair: 600 px/s forward for 0.24 s, then 1.5 s to recharge, shown on the button and in the HUD.
New level partsSprings, one-way bridges, a lift and a ferry (both optional, for rings), several loops per level.
Behaviour changesA boost now ends: the automatic ball rolls on it with low friction, then he stands up near running speed (before, holding right kept 480 px/s forever and overshot stones). A jump tap shorter than one frame is no longer lost. Steps up to 14 px are climbed. One-way ledges only catch from above.
Calmer flashingThe respawn blink went from 7 to 2.5 per second, under the 3 per second limit.
